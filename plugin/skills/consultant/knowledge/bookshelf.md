# The bookshelf: frameworks the consultant teaches, and where they come from

The consultant's knowledge files (offer, money model, conversion, traffic, constraints) are built on the
ideas below. When you use one, **name the book and author in one line** so the owner can go deeper:
"This is the value equation from Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers*." Summarize in your own words.

**Quotes:** a short quote (one sentence) makes advice land, but only quote when you've confirmed the
exact wording with a web search of a reliable source (the publisher, the author's own site, a
reputable interview). Otherwise paraphrase: "Hormozi's point is that…". Never quote long passages,
never invent a quote, and never speak as the author.

| Book (author) | The idea to use | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| *$100M Offers* (Alex Hormozi) | **Value equation**: value rises with the dream outcome and the perceived likelihood of getting it, and falls with the time it takes and the effort or sacrifice. Build a "Grand Slam Offer": list every obstacle the customer faces, turn each into a solution, then bundle, add bonuses, scarcity, urgency and a guarantee until comparing you to a competitor makes no sense. | Price objections, "we're a commodity", low close rates (`offer.md`) |
| *$100M Leads* (Alex Hormozi) | **The core four** ways to get leads: warm outreach, cold outreach, content, paid ads; then "lead getters" (referrals, affiliates, employees, agencies). Start with warm outreach; do one channel well before adding another. A lead magnet should solve a narrow problem and reveal a bigger one. | "How do I get more customers", traffic layer (`traffic.md`) |
| *$100M Money Models* (Alex Hormozi) | Sequence offers to get cash in early: attraction offer, upsell, downsell, continuity. Aim for the first 30 days of a customer to pay for the cost of getting them. | Cash-hungry growth, ad spend that doesn't pay back (`money-model.md`) |
| *The E-Myth Revisited* (Michael E. Gerber) | Most small businesses are started by technicians who end up working *in* the business, not *on* it. Document the work as systems so it runs without you. | Owner does everything, can't take a week off, stage 2 to 3 (`growth-stages.md`) |
| *Profit First* (Mike Michalowicz) | Take profit first: split every deposit into accounts (profit, owner pay, tax, operating) by set percentages, and run the business on what's left. | Profitable on paper but always broke; pairs with `tax set-aside` |
| *Clockwork* (Mike Michalowicz) | Find the one function that matters most to the business ("queen bee role"), protect it, and move the rest of the work off the owner. | Owner is the bottleneck |
| *Traction* (Gino Wickman) | EOS: a short vision, quarterly "rocks" (3 to 7 priorities), a weekly scorecard of 5 to 15 numbers, and a weekly meeting that solves issues. | Team of 3+, priorities drift, the 90-day plan needs a rhythm |
| *The Goal* (Eliyahu M. Goldratt) | Theory of constraints: a business only goes as fast as its tightest bottleneck; find it, exploit it, subordinate everything else to it, then elevate it. | "Why am I stuck" (`constraints.md`) |
| *Building a StoryBrand* (Donald Miller) | The customer is the hero, the business is the guide. Website and ads should say clearly what you offer, how it makes their life better, and what to do next. | Website and ads that don't convert, confusing messaging |
| *Never Split the Difference* (Chris Voss) | Tactical empathy: label their emotions ("It sounds like you've been burned before"), mirror, ask calibrated "how" and "what" questions, and aim for "that's right". | Sales calls, negotiating with landlords, suppliers, clients; collections |
| *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick) | Ask about people's real past behaviour, not their opinion of your idea. "When did that last happen? What did you do about it?" Compliments are not data. | Testing a new offer before building it; pairs with `roast-room` |
| *Good to Great* (Jim Collins) | The hedgehog concept: what you can be best at, what drives your economic engine, and what you're passionate about. | Choosing what to stop doing |
| *Oversubscribed* (Daniel Priestley) | Demand should exceed supply: launch in campaigns, build a waitlist, and let scarcity be real. | Service businesses raising prices or launching a new offer |
| *Scaling Up* (Verne Harnish) | People, strategy, execution, cash: the four decisions; the cash conversion cycle (get paid faster, pay later). | Growing past ~10 staff, cash squeezed while growing |
| *This Is Marketing* (Seth Godin) | Pick the smallest viable audience and serve them so well they talk about you. | Trying to market to "everyone" |

Canadian context the books don't cover: GST/HST registration at $30k, CPP on self-employment, provincial
employment standards, and the regulator rules for health and other professions. Run those through the
`accountant`, `hiring` and `lawyer` skills.
