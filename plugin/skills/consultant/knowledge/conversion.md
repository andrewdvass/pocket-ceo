# Layer 3 — Conversion

How the offer gets sold. Fixed after the offer, because a great sales process for a commoditized offer just makes you better at losing price fights.

## Cold-to-warm is the scaling rule

The single most important conversion principle for anyone trying to grow:

Owners with an audience unconsciously sell to people who already know them — skipping credibility, context, and proof. It converts fine on warm traffic and collapses the moment they buy attention. If someone says "my ads don't work but my organic converts great," this is usually why.

Rebuild the pitch as if the buyer has never heard of them. Warm audiences still convert; cold ones start to.

## The structured webinar / VSL

The most common missing asset in businesses stuck under a few million. Owners resist it because they associate structure with "bro marketing." The counter:

And: think of everything you bought last month — how many felt like a sale? Someone thought hard about each of those purchases. They just did it well.

The second objection is length. A three-to-four-hour unstructured session isn't generous, it's a tax — you lose half the audience before the offer, which is a disservice to the people who needed it. Forty-five structured minutes has a higher value-per-second.

**The structure:**

1. **Big promise** — why staying on is worth it. This comes first, before the introduction.
2. **Who am I** — the short credibility story. Why listen to you.
3. **Old way / new way** — your core transformation. I did this, it failed, I discovered X, I implemented it, here's the outcome. Then: it didn't just work for me, it worked for A, B, and C (hitting the different avatars).
4. **The three problems** — the biggest concerns your buyers have. Each one runs the same four-beat pattern:
 - **What they think**
 - **Why that's wrong** — "I used to believe this too"
 - **What's actually right**
 - **Proof** — stats, stories, screenshots, testimonials. As many forms as you can.
5. **The offer**
6. **FAQ** — see below.

Visuals matter: slides, screenshots, and on-screen proof beat a talking head, partly because a chunk of the audience is half-listening in the background.

## The FAQ block

An underrated conversion asset, and it costs almost nothing to build.

Pull every question that comes through DMs, sales calls, and support. Bucket them. Answer them at the end of the webinar in **descending order of frequency** — the one 40% of people ask goes first. Put the question on screen, then answer it.

This is what kills the need for endless one-on-one hand-holding. The one-on-one conversations exist because the objection isn't being handled up front. Handle it up front.

Keep going as long as people are on. If they're still watching, keep answering.

## Structured discovery

For phone and consultative sales, the questions do the differentiating. Don't ask only the logistics questions needed to quote — ask the preference questions that expose your advantages:

- → they never thought about it → sets up your certification tier
- → sets up the done-for-you inclusion
- → sets up insurance
- → sets up the price guarantee

You're listening and building the recommendation as they answer. Then present the premium tier with the elements they just told you they care about, and step down only on budget signals. Each question is a seed of doubt planted as genuine service.

## Separate delivery from selling

A very common muddle: the owner teaches inside their pitch and pitches inside their delivery, so both do neither well.

Delivery content becomes marketing content (see `traffic.md`). Pitches stay pitches. Untangling these two frequently doubles both.

## Speed and separation

**Speed to contact** is a live constraint at stage 4 and above. Track it before it slips.

**Separation before the close** improves conversion at in-person and event sales. Find any natural gap in the process — a 20-minute scan, a wait, a break — and fill it with a VSL. Then close immediately after. If it feels too fast, add a scheduled second appointment with the VSL in between. Two versions to test in order:

- **V1:** experience + VSL → close same day
- **V2:** experience + VSL → book appointment → VSL again → close

## Sales collateral is a stage-5 deliverable

At 10–19 people, the founder can't be the only person who can sell. Sales aids and materials — webinar, VSL, scripts, FAQ bank — plus **weekly game tape between sales, CS, and marketing** are the graduation criteria. Systemized sales training becomes the constraint at stage 6, especially if the plan involves running many events.

## Handling the "I don't want to seem salesy" owner

Frequent in mission-led businesses, and it's a conversion constraint like any other. Three moves:

1. **Reframe selling as education.** 
2. **Point at the cost of the current approach.** Every hour spent on a call that a structured asset could have handled is an hour not spent on someone who needed it.
3. **Separate the person from the business.** They can be in it for whatever reason they like. The business needs profit to grow, and growth is how they reach more people.
