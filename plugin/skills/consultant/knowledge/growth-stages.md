# Growth stages

Use this to tell the owner where they are and what usually breaks next. The point is reassurance plus focus:
their problems are normal for their stage, and only one or two of them matter right now.

| Stage | Roughly | What it feels like | The one thing to fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Proving it | $0 to first 10 paying customers | Selling to friends, unsure what to charge | Get 10 people to pay full price for one clear offer |
| 2. Owner does everything | up to ~$150k/yr, 0 to 1 helpers | Fully booked or broke, nothing in between | A repeatable way to get customers that isn't word of mouth |
| 3. First hires | ~$150k to $500k/yr, 2 to 5 people | Owner is the bottleneck on every decision | Write down how the main work is done so someone else can do it |
| 4. Small team | ~$500k to $2M/yr, 5 to 15 people | Quality slips when the owner isn't there | A manager or lead who owns delivery; owner moves to sales and marketing |
| 5. Real company | $2M+/yr, 15+ people | Departments, cash flow planning, hiring is constant | Leaders for each area, monthly numbers the owner actually reads |

## How to use it
1. Place them by revenue AND team size. If they disagree (big revenue, tiny team), say so: it usually means the owner is overloaded.
2. Name the one thing to fix for their stage, tied to something they told you.
3. Say what usually breaks at the next stage, in one sentence, so they can see it coming.
4. Do not hand a 3-person business a plan built for a 30-person company.
