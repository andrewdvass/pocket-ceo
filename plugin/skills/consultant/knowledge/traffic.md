# Layer 4 — Traffic

How people find out. Fixed last, because volume into a broken offer or leaky funnel just raises the price of learning nothing.

## The ad machine

The organizing idea. Build the business so its normal operation generates its own marketing.

The loop: **you advertise once → a customer comes in → they have a great experience → the experience is documented → the documentation becomes the advertising → that brings the next customer.** After the initial push, the snowball rolls itself.

Practical ways to close the loop:

- **Rebate for content.** Price includes a rebate paid when the customer sends a video of their experience, with permission to use it. Tell them to talk about the experience, not the money. Add the rebate cost into the price.
- **Unlockable bonus for proof.** Gate a desirable extra behind showing themselves using the product. Doubles as first-week activation: every customer produces an ad, and every customer gets a quick win. With 200 customers a launch, that's 200 ads.
- **Before/after capture as standard operating procedure.** For any physical or visual service, have the on-site lead capture before and after at both ends of the job, cut together, with the review card at the end. Post it every time.
- **Q&A as a content engine.** If delivery generates 30–50 questions a week, that's 30–50 posts a week. Repetition doesn't matter — audiences don't perceive it as repetition. *Dave Ramsey has answered the same question for forty-five years.*
- **Green-screen reaction posts.** Pull a customer question on screen behind you and answer it. Never open with "a lot of people have been asking me this" — that's a dead hook.

Winners from organic become paid ads. Organic is the free testing ground.

## Creative volume

The most common gap between a business that's stuck and one that scales.

Going from 4 creatives to 80+ per launch is routinely a top-two lever. Where the volume comes from:

- **Founder ads:** take your 20 best-performing ads ever and reshoot each in 4 different settings — different shirt, background, location. That's 80 from existing proven material.
- **Community screenshots:** posts, wins, and comments from your group, with highlights added.
- **Customer-generated images** of the outcome. Weird ones perform.
- **Clips from lives, Q&As, and podcasts** you already recorded.
- **Your camera roll.** Literally. Run all of them.
- **AI-generated statics**, which cost minutes.

Two rules that save money:

- **Copy stays, creative changes.** Once the message resonates, the work is getting it in front of different people in different ways.
- **Video is high-variance.** Your best ads will be video and your worst ads will be video. Good video beats images; bad video loses to images. Run both.

## Spend cadence

For launch and event-based models:

| Phase | Share of spend | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 5–10% | Test creative only |
| Weeks 2–3 | flat, scaling up | Spend as much as the platform will take |
| Final week | 60–70% | The bulk |
| Final 3 days | ~50% of total | The spike |

Owners consistently under-spend at the end and finish a launch thinking "I should have spent more." Pair the cadence with the 30-day recovery number from `money-model.md` so the ceiling is a decision rather than a flinch.

Optimize campaigns for **purchases**, not leads.

## Levels of awareness

Eugene Schwartz's five levels, and the reason ads stop working when you scale them. Every market splits into five groups, from the smallest and warmest to the largest and coldest:

**Most aware → product aware → solution aware → problem aware → unaware**

Most businesses write only product-aware and offer-aware ads. Those work at low spend and then break, because the addressable pool is tiny. To scale, write **pain-aware and problem-aware hooks** — they open the targeting up dramatically.

If CPA triples when spend goes up, this is usually the cause.

## A hundred ponds, not oceans

Counterintuitive and important:

Running two ads at everyone with the same message caps you. Scale comes from going *more* specific — many sub-avatar callouts, each with its own hook, rather than one universal message. Segmenting "mothers" and "grandmothers" is a start; go further down.

## Event-based marketing

For high-ticket, high-trust sales — health, wealth, professional services — events routinely beat funnels.

**Attend other people's events.** Any decent-sized city has health, wealth, or business events weekly. Bring the demonstration, run the play, go home with the pipeline. Rough math on a 300-person event: get a third to take the demo (100), convert 25% (25 sales). At high LTV that's a year of revenue from one weekend.

**The cadence is the strategy.** Try many, keep the winners. Then you're a sponsor there every year and the calendar becomes the marketing plan.

**Run your own** only once the attending version is working — building your own venue and audience is two steps ahead, and seasonality will bite (one physician discovered his market empties out five months a year).

**Giveaways are the attraction offer.** Give away a full year of the premium service. It's cheap in real terms, and it lets you state the real price in a non-pressuring way — — so there's no sticker shock at the close. Everyone who enters also gets something real, which makes referring a favor rather than a favor asked.

**Half-day workshops** work for high-ticket. Charge a small amount ($99) — it filters out the riff-raff and puts a card on file, which makes the close easier.

## Referral engines

**Make referring socially profitable, not just financially.** A $500 credit means nothing to a wealthy customer. Giving *their friend* a free VIP upgrade makes the referrer look connected and generous. Same for partners: beats a cash kickback, because it makes the partner look good to their own client.

**BAM FAM — book a meeting from a meeting.** At every appointment, set the next one and ask Not "do you have any referrals" — assume it. Build it into the offer as a stated benefit ("you get to bring a guest to each of your sessions"), back it with the real evidence that people do better at health and behavior change together, and the marginal cost is near zero.

**Find the wow moment** and ask there. Somewhere in the customer journey is the point where the result lands — 90 days into a health protocol, the first completed project. Anchor the referral ask to it.

**Newsletter PS.** Low-friction, no-pressure: a postscript announcing the quarterly giveaway. Costs one line.

## Partner and channel leverage

The single highest-leverage traffic move for local and B2B2C service businesses: **stop selling to individuals, sell to the aggregator.**

The worked example: a moving company selling to individual realtors was told to go to the *brokerage*. One brokerage is 20 agents; each agent sells ~4 houses a year; that's ~60 jobs a year from one conversation. 

Then go further — **get integrated into their process.** Be in the packet they hand every client. 

Show up in person once a partner responds. Bring donuts, meet the whole office, extend the offer to every agent, use the existing happy client by name. Expect the next constraint to be relationship management, and plan the hire.

## Outreach mechanics

- **Personalize the first message with AI.** Feed the list in, have it research each prospect, generate a personalized opener, spot-check, send. It beats a generic opener and it keeps carriers from flagging you for sending identical messages at volume.
- **Use a local area code** and test rich messaging (blue bubble) — it costs more per message and often wins on response rate.
- **Follow, then DM.** Following before DMing raises response rates; once they follow back, the message limits lift.
- **Funnel every platform to one conversion point.** If Instagram DMs are where you convert, YouTube and TikTok bios and descriptions should say "DM me on Instagram." Don't run parallel conversion surfaces.

## Small mechanics worth keeping

- **Delay the CTA banner ~3 seconds** on video ads. Let the hook land first; the traffic quality is measurably better.
- **Spiff reviews through the front-line lead, not the owner.** Coming from the crew rather than the owner, it converts far better. Pay out monthly to whoever earned the most.
- **Video testimonials from partners** are worth more than customer reviews for partner-driven channels.
- **Local social following is a partner asset.** Announcing a new partnership to a local audience is free promotion for them, and it's a closing argument for the next partner.
