# Output: the diagnosis and 90-day plan

The deliverable. Aim for something an owner could act on Monday — dense, ranked, specific to their numbers.

Use their language and their numbers throughout. If a section would read identically for any business in their industry, it's wrong.

---

## Structure

### 1. The read (3–5 sentences)

What the business is, what's actually true about it, and the one-line diagnosis. Lead with the finding, not a summary of their intake — they know what they told you.

State the **constraint classification** explicitly: demand or supply, and why.

### 2. Where you are: growth stage

Growth stage (from knowledge/growth-stages.md) and team size. The constraints live right now, tied to things they said. What's coming next stage.

### 3. The plan, back to front

Only the layers the diagnosis implicates. Say which you're skipping and why — 

For each layer: what's wrong, what to do instead, and why it works. Show the arithmetic wherever a number can be computed from what they gave you.

**Offer** → **Money model** → **Conversion** → **Traffic**

### 4. The levers, ranked

The section that makes the plan usable. Rank everything, size it, and be explicit about what not to bother with.

| 2 | | | | |
| 3 | | | | |

### 5. The 90-day blueprint

Concrete, sequenced, with a first action for each. Weeks are better than months.

**Weeks 1–2 —** the offer and pricing changes, because everything downstream depends on them
**Weeks 3–4 —** conversion assets built
**Weeks 5–8 —** traffic turned up, first tests run
**Weeks 9–12 —** measure, double down, second test

Include the **one test** that would most change the trajectory, framed honestly as a test, with how to de-risk it (usually: run it as a split against the current version at the same price so the only variable is the thing being tested).

### 6. What breaks next

The constraint that shows up if this works. Owners find this valuable and nobody gives it to them. 

### 7. The verdict

**On track**, **Stuck** or **At risk**, in bold, with the reason.

The verdict is about the operator as much as the plan. Say which one is the issue.

Legitimate reasons for Stuck or At risk:
- **Not nailed yet** — the model isn't finished; scaling it would multiply an unfinished thing
- **The operator won't let go** — the plan requires giving up something they're emotionally attached to (answering every DM, doing all delivery, being in every sale)
- **Too far from the interesting version** — the plan works but the business is several steps from the model that actually compounds

If it's Stuck or At risk, say exactly what would move it to On track. That's the useful part.

## Tone

Direct and warm. Blunt about the business, kind about the person. It's fine to say "I don't love this" about a weak part — then immediately work the problem.

Explain *why* each tactic works, with a concrete analogy where one helps, so the owner can generalize later instead of just following instructions.

No hype, no manufactured urgency, no guru cadence. When something is genuinely a coin flip, say so and say how you'd de-risk it.
