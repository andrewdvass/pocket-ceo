# Which laws apply, by province (where to look)

Use this to know **which law to check**, then confirm the current rule on CanLII.org or the official
provincial legislation site before answering. Last checked: **2026-10-14**.

## Federal (everywhere)
- **Privacy:** PIPEDA applies to private-sector businesses in provinces without their own substantially
  similar law, and to cross-border data. (Alberta, BC and Quebec have their own; several provinces
  have health-information laws.)
- **Anti-spam:** CASL (Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation) governs commercial emails and texts: consent,
  identification and an unsubscribe in every message.
- **Competition Act:** false or misleading advertising, including fake reviews and drip pricing.
- **Federally regulated businesses** (banks, airlines, telecom, interprovincial transport) follow the
  Canada Labour Code instead of provincial employment law.

## Employment standards (minimum wage, hours, vacation, termination notice)
| Province | Law |
|---|---|
| Alberta | Employment Standards Code |
| British Columbia | Employment Standards Act |
| Saskatchewan | The Saskatchewan Employment Act |
| Manitoba | The Employment Standards Code |
| Ontario | Employment Standards Act, 2000 (also: restrictions on non-compete clauses for employees) |
| Quebec | Act respecting labour standards (CNESST) + Civil Code of Québec |
| New Brunswick | Employment Standards Act |
| Nova Scotia | Labour Standards Code |
| Prince Edward Island | Employment Standards Act |
| Newfoundland and Labrador | Labour Standards Act |
| Territories | Each territory's Employment Standards Act / Labour Standards Act |

Common-law reasonable notice on termination is usually more than the statutory minimum in common-law
provinces: flag this when someone is firing a long-tenured employee without a written termination clause.

## Private-sector privacy
| Province | Law |
|---|---|
| Alberta | Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) + Health Information Act for custodians (PIPA is under review in 2026; no replacement law yet) |
| British Columbia | Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) |
| Quebec | Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (as amended by Law 25) |
| Ontario | PIPEDA for most businesses + Personal Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA) for health custodians |
| Others | PIPEDA + the province's health-information act where one exists |

## Consumer protection (selling to individuals)
Each province has a consumer protection act covering contracts, cancellation rights for direct and
internet sales, gift cards, and unfair practices. Examples: Alberta Consumer Protection Act; Ontario
Consumer Protection Act, 2002 (still the law in force as of Oct 2026; the Consumer Protection Act, 2023 passed
but has not been proclaimed in force yet: check e-Laws for a start date); BC Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act; Quebec Consumer Protection Act.

## Limitation periods (how long to sue)
Usually **2 years** from when the claim was discovered in most provinces (e.g. Alberta, Ontario, BC),
with variations; Quebec generally 3 years. Confirm before advising on a deadline.

## Business registration and names
Sole proprietors using a name other than their own usually must register a trade name provincially.
Corporations can be provincial or federal (Corporations Canada).

## Regulated professions
Advertising, testimonials, and client-record rules come from the profession's regulator (for example,
a provincial College of Physicians and Surgeons, College of Naturopaths, Law Society, real-estate
council). Always check the regulator's current advertising standard before approving marketing copy.
